
Asignment:
	-Prepare a 3–4-minute presentation that makes an argument about the representation of war across 
	The Iliad, Master and Commander, and Taking Fire. One slide per person.

Point of View:
	Perspective and the human cost.

Guiding Question:
	-How does each work’s choice of perspective shape whose experience of war matters to us?

Points of Entry:
	-The treatment and voices of captives in Book 1; Hector, Andromache, and their child in Book 6; 
	-Achilles’ reflections on the value of his life in Book 9; 
	-The depiction of injury, ordinary sailors, and shipboard community in Master and Commander; 
	-The experiences made visible through soldiers’ footage and testimony in, Taking Fire.


Response:
	In the Iliad the war between the Trojans and the Achaeans is portrayed from both sides. As such, this creates a level of sympathy for both sides that is not usually portrayed in media about wars. It goes to humanize both sides, but in particular the Trojans who, since the war is taking place in their own territory, are shown ripped out of there domestic life defending there home. The suffering of, specifically the women, but overall the trojan people, who face slavery and other horrors if defeated. At the same time it shows the deep depression of the losing Greeks, who struggle under a greedy, incompetent commander. Overall my point is how when you shift the perspective of a war from the view of one side, who is often clouded by grief and rage, too both, the suffering shows a more realistic, sad, and humanizing view of wars and how they cause suffering no matter which side you stand on.
