		religeous views. He got to Narragansett Bay and founded Providence Colony on the foundations of religous freedom.

		Portsmouth in 1639. They then subsiquently split into Portsmouth and Newport, with Portsmouth having there governing
		document, the Portsmouth compact. In 1642 Samuel Gorton bought some land near Providence after becoming disatisfied
		with there conflicting religous beliefs between Massechuses, and founded Providence Plantation. In 1644 Portsmouth,
		Providence, Newport, joined together and became the Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantation. Samuel Gorten

	Religeon:
		Since all of the original founding members of Rhode Island fled Massechuses on the idea of there restrictive religous
		government, Religous freedom was a central idea in there colonial Government. Roger Williams himself was a puritan
		Minister, before reforming to baptist building the first Baptist Church in America in Providence. Rhode Island had no
		official colony church or religon, as guided by there belief in religous freedom. This also included seperation of church
		and state, both of these things that would later go on to be part of the US constitution. In 1776 there where 26 Baptist, 
		11 quaker, 9 Congregational, 5 Episcopal, 1 Jewish, 1 New light Congregational, 1 Presbyterian, 1 Sandemanian place of
		worships.

	Slavery:
		
		While owning slaves was made illegal in 1652, the first british colony to make this rule, this was not enforced
		and Rhode Island was heavily involved in the slave trade. Slavery was made popular in 1688, although with no 
		evidence of any laws making it legal. This let them prosper in a triangle trade with the Carribean and Africa
		which I will talk about later. They were not only a item to be traded, but they also held up the economy, working
		on wharfs and in the few farms that supplied the colony with food or making goods to trade. Due to the large
		population of quakers, there was a push for outlawing slavery, which only really started in 1772, and in 1774
		they would be the first british colony to make illegal the importation of slavery. In 1774 slaves made up 
		6.3% of the total population in Rhode Island. During the year of 1784 Rhode Island emacipated all slaves
		with a big push by the quaker led government.

	Economics:
		As mentioned before, Rhode Island was heavily involved in the triangle trade. Slaves where bought in Africa, who
		where then sold for sugar in the Carribean, which was distilled into Rum in Rhode Island. That Rum was taken to
		African slave markets and traded for slaves, a process that was very profitable to Colonial Rhode Island. They
		also traded alot in martine goods in general, selling locally produced salt, making ships to sell, and fishing for
		fish to salt and sell. They did also farm in Rhode Island, but this food mostly stayed in the colony and was eaten
		instead of sold locally. Overall there Maritine economy relied heavily on trade and locally produced goods.
