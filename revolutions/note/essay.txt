



	Marie-Joseph Paul Yves Roch Gilbert du Motier, Marquis de La Fayette (whom I will abreviate to Lafayette), was born to Michel Lafayette and Marie Lafayette on the 6th of September 1757. The Lafayette family at that point was one of the oldest noble families in France still around, they had a long militaristic history and all the men had a long standing tradition of enlisting in the military. As a result however Lafayette's lost his father at the age of 2; The Marquis de Lafayette was killed on August 1st 1759, hit by a Anglo-German Cannonball during battle. This left Lafayette as the current Marquis of Lafayette thus his title. He grew up very rich, although fatherless and not with his mother until the age of 11 when in 1768 he would go to live with her and his Grandfather in the Luxemburg Palace. Around this time he would attend school at the College du Plessis a division of the University of Paris, and got enrolled in a Musketeer training program by his Grandfather. Unfortuaneley again for Lafayette his mother would die on April 3 1770 and leave him a inheritance of 2500 livre, and his uncle would later die and leave him 120000 livre annually, basicly setting him up for life. While he was only 14 he would get his first military position as an 'Sous Lieutenent' in the Musketeers, and at the same age he was introduced to his future wife Adrienne de La Fayette who was 12. The marrige contract was signed in 1773 and he went to live in Versailles with his father in law, having an arranged, yet happy, marrige in 1774 and be married until she died in 1807. Because of the move he would switch schools, and fun fact he was classmates with the future king Charles X. His father in law would later have him switch to be apart of the Noailles Dragoons, a division which he commanded, making him Lafayettes superior commander and boss, yikes. So hopefully this information will help you paint a picture of young Lafayette; while he faced many hardships and was orphaned overall he was living the dream french noble life, he was high ranking and had enough money and power to be comfortable for life. 
	Yet Lafayette wasnt a heartless snobby monster (inspite of his frenchness), in 1757 he was training at Metz with his unit, a job which sounds boring but involved eating dinner with other rich noblemen, one of which just so happened to be Charles Ruffec. Even though he was nobility, Charles Ruffec was a strong believer in liberty and discussed the American Revolution with a young Lafayette who found alot of sympathy with the American Rebels. Part of this might have been because of his strong dislike for the british: that of which was shared with most french people, but especially Lafayette because they had cannonballed his father to death. Although not all of the nobillty in his circle shared his sentiment, he soon after dined with the duke of Gloucester, who happened too look down on the Americans and mock them, although it is from him that Lafayette learned that the Americans where recruiting. In September of 1775 he turned 18, and as a wedding gift from his father in law, was promoted to a Captain, soon after his first child Henriette would be born that December. Around the birth of his daughter Lafayette really started to open his eyes, he would come to the realisation that the values of the Americans matched his own and would be determined from that point on to help them out. The Americans came to France, and there they where recruiting French Military officers to the American Army to fight agaisnt the British; when Lafayette found out he demanded to go along with them. The Americans happily accepted and enlisted him as a Major General on December 7th 1776. The British government found out about this however and threatened war agaisnt France, maybe more scarily though his Father in Law found out and was so very pissed at Lafayette (he was still living with him at this time too).
